Microscopic simulation is widely used to analyse signalised intersections, yet the default behaviour models of simulators such as SUMO assume lane-disciplined, rule-compliant traffic. Mixed car--motorcycle traffic in Vietnamese cities exhibits non-normative behaviours such as swarm red-light running, early starts, lateral encroachment, right-turn-lane blocking or sidewalk riding in queues, and illegal left turns in which riders wait inside the junction box. Calibrating a large parameter space by hand to reproduce these behaviours is laborious and hard to trace. This thesis proposes a workflow in which a locally hosted large language model (LLM) generates and iteratively refines a driver-behaviour rule specification consisting of static vehicle-type parameters and conditional rules executed through TraCI. The LLM only produces rules outside the simulation loop; the rules are executed by SUMO and a TraCI controller. Every specification is validated against a schema and a source-documented parameter space before it is compiled and simulated. The workflow is applied to the Le Van Luong -- Hoang Dao Thuy intersection in Hanoi during the morning peak, with illegal left turns as the focus scenario. To date, the whole workflow has been implemented: a reproducible simulation network (including continuous right-turn lanes, a bollard-separated segment and a violator-only sidewalk lane), feasibility experiments on in-junction waiting, right-turn-lane blocking, sidewalk riding and wrong-way riding (the last one deferred), the validator, the compiler, a controller with five conditional rules, the M1--M10 metric suite with an aggregate score, an evaluation harness with a shared simulation budget, the LLM loop and the Optuna baseline. The Optuna baseline has used its full budget of 36 runs, whereas the two LLM campaigns (parameter tuning on a fixed structure, and generation of structure and parameters) have completed two of three iterations; because the machine running them has no local LLM yet, every specification of these campaigns was produced by a stand-in model (Claude Sonnet) and is therefore not a result of the proposed method. None of the 28 specifications evaluated so far passes the eligibility criteria, because the base model is overloaded at the proxy demand; these interim results do not support any comparison between the LLM and Optuna.
